Whodunit Daily – Privacy Policy

Effective 16 November 2026

Whodunit Daily is a detective logic puzzle game for Android made by Normalist (“we”). This policy explains what happens to your data when you use the app. Our Terms of Service cover the rest.

In short

  • No account and no sign-in.
  • Your progress and settings stay on your device. We run no servers of our own.
  • The app shows ads from Google AdMob. Google collects some data to show and measure those ads, as described below.
  • We use Google Analytics for Firebase to see how the game is played, for example which cases players solve or give up on. It never includes your name, email or any account. You can turn it off in Settings.
  • “Remove ads” is an optional one-time purchase through Google Play. We never see your payment details.

Data kept on your device

The app saves your results for each case (stars, time, hints used and wrong accusations), the case you are in the middle of, your daily streak, your hints, how far you have opened the Casebook, whether you bought “Remove ads”, the timing of full-screen ads, and your sound, music, vibration, auto-cross and usage statistics settings. This data is stored in the app’s private storage on your device and is never sent to us. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data removes it. If Android backup is turned on, Android may include it in your device backup to your Google account, under Google’s terms.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The app shows a banner at the bottom of the case screen, optional rewarded ads (for an extra hint, or to reopen a case after three wrong accusations, only when you choose to watch one) and, after your first few solved cases, an occasional full-screen ad when you leave a solved case. Ads are provided by Google’s Mobile Ads SDK, which automatically collects and shares with Google:

  • your IP address, which may be used to estimate your general location;
  • interactions with the app and ads, such as app launches, taps and video views;
  • diagnostic information about the app’s and the SDK’s performance;
  • device identifiers: the Android advertising ID and app set ID.

Google uses this data for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. It is encrypted in transit. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the app asks for your consent before ads are personalised; you can change your choice at any time in Settings → Privacy choices. Your consent choice is stored on your device.

You can reset or delete your advertising ID in your Android settings (Settings → Privacy → Ads, or Settings → Google → Ads, depending on the device). Learn more in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy.

Without an internet connection the app requests no ads and the game stays fully playable.

Usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

To learn which parts of the game work and where players get stuck, the app sends usage events to Google Analytics for Firebase:

  • game events: cases started, solved, failed or given up (with the case number, grid size, number of clues, time, stars, hints used and wrong accusations), tutorial steps, results shared, rewarded and full-screen ads shown, and “Remove ads” purchases started, completed or restored;
  • error reports: when something goes wrong in the app, the kind of error, a short error message and the place in the game’s code where it happened (never anything you typed or stored);
  • information Firebase adds on its own: an app instance ID (a random ID for this installation), the device model, Android and app versions, your country or region estimated from your IP address, and when the app was first opened and used;
  • on devices that allow it, the Android advertising ID, which links these statistics with the ad measurement described above.

We see these statistics only in aggregate, in the Firebase console. Google processes them for us, keeps the event-level data for at most 14 months and uses them under the Google Privacy Policy; in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, your consent choice above also applies to their use for advertising. To stop sending them, turn off Settings → Usage stats.

Remove ads (in-app purchase)

“Remove ads” is a one-time purchase that turns off the banner and the ads between cases; rewarded ads stay available when you choose to watch one. Google Play handles the payment, so we never receive your card or payment details. The app only learns from Google Play whether you own the purchase, keeps that on your device, and checks it again with Google Play when it starts. The Google Payments Privacy Notice covers the payment itself.

Sharing your result

When you tap Share after a Daily case, the app hands a short text (the case number, your time, stars and streak, with no solution) to the Android share sheet. It goes only where you choose to send it.

Permissions

  • Google Play billing – for the optional “Remove ads” purchase.
  • Install referrer – lets Google Analytics for Firebase know which store page or campaign led to the install.

These come with Google’s Mobile Ads SDK and the libraries it uses:

  • Internet and network state – to load ads.
  • Advertising ID and ad services – for ad measurement, as described above.
  • Wake lock and foreground service – for the SDK’s short background tasks.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them.

Your choices and rights

We hold no account or contact data about you. Usage statistics are tied only to a random app instance ID, which we cannot link to you; turn off Settings → Usage stats to stop sending them, or uninstall the app to drop the ID. To remove the data on your device, uninstall the app or clear its data. For data collected by Google for ads, use the controls above or contact Google.

Changes

If this policy changes, we will update this page and its effective date.
